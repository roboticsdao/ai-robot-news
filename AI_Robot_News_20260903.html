Escudo Digital — Critical flaws in Unitree's G1 robot enable remote takeover and chain‑reaction infections

Security researcher Olivier Laflamme discovered two critical vulnerabilities in the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, priced at roughly $20,000, which allow remote administrator takeover without owner authorization. The first flaw involves an AI-based feature that improperly verifies file saving paths, enabling arbitrary file execution when combined with another program. The second flaw permits remote Bluetooth attacks by exploiting a decryption service on the manufacturer's servers, allowing unauthenticated users to obtain encryption keys. Laflamme demonstrated that a compromised robot could potentially infect other nearby units in a chain reaction. Unitree cooperated by fixing the main verification flaw within two months and awarded the researcher a $5,000 bounty.

总结：安全研究员奥利维尔·拉弗拉姆（Olivier Laflamme）在售价约2万美元的宇树G1人形机器人中发现了两个严重漏洞，允许在未经所有者授权的情况下远程获取管理员控制权。 第一个漏洞涉及一个未能正确验证文件保存路径的人工智能功能，结合另一个程序后可执行任意文件。 第二个漏洞利用制造商服务器上的解密服务进行远程蓝牙攻击，允许未认证用户获取加密密钥。 拉弗拉姆演示了被攻陷的机器人可能会通过连锁反应感染附近其他单元。 宇树在两个月内修复了主要的验证漏洞并予以配合，向该研究员颁发了5,000美元的奖金。
