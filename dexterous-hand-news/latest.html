Tech Briefs — Color-Changing Tactile Sensor Allows Robots to “See” and Touch in Real-Time

Engineers at Queen Mary University of London have developed a color-changing tactile sensor that allows robots to see and touch in real-time. Invented by postdoctoral researcher Giacomo Sasso, the device transforms invisible forces into dynamic color patterns using a soft sensing surface. This enables high-resolution contact, strain, and pressure maps to appear instantly and be captured by a standard USB camera without complex reconstruction algorithms. The technology can be applied in precision manufacturing for assembling micro-scale components, in healthcare for giving external prosthetic limbs a richer sense of touch, and in surgical systems for distinguishing healthy tissue from abnormal tissue. By embedding sensing directly into the material, the system avoids the traditional trade-off between high spatial resolution and low computational latency. Co-authors from Italian universities including Florence, Trieste, and Trento collaborated on the research, merging soft robotics and material science.

总结：伦敦玛丽女王大学的工程师开发了一种变色触觉传感器，可让机器人实时进行“看见”与触摸。 该项创新由该校工程与材料科学学院的博士后研究员Giacomo Sasso发明，其原理是通过柔软的感应表面将无形的作用力转化为动态的色彩图案。 这使得接触、应变和压力的的高分辨率图谱能够瞬间呈现，并可通过标准USBgetCamera直接读取，无需复杂的重建算法。 该技术可用于精密制造中组装微型组件、医疗领域中为外部假肢提供更丰富的触感，以及手术系统中通过材料的色彩反应区分健康与异常组织。 通过将传感功能直接嵌入材料本身，该系统克服了高空间分辨率与低计算延迟之间的传统权衡。 来自意大利佛罗伦萨大学、里雅斯特大学和特伦托大学的合著者共同参与了这项研究，融合了软体机器人与材料科学领域。
