- **[2026.10.16] Robotics & Automation News — Tesla patent reveals touch-sensitive skin for robot hands**
  English: Tesla has filed a US patent application, US 2026/0310299 A1, published on October 8, for a touch-sensitive skin that can be molded around robot fingers and palms to detect contact, force, and pressure. The application describes printing tactile sensors onto flat, flexible materials before heat-forming them into three-dimensional shapes. The proposed sensor uses two flexible substrate layers with conductive regions separated by a compressible layer, utilizing capacitive and piezoresistive approaches. Substrate materials listed include thermoplastic polyurethane, PET, and polyimide. Tesla emphasizes manufacturability through established processes like screen printing, laser cutting, conductive paste deposition, and thermoforming to enable production at scale. While the filing does not specify use in the Optimus humanoid robot, it notes potential applications in robot fingertips, palms, and end-effectors to advance dexterous manipulation.

- **[2026.10.15] 36Kr — Embodied Intelligence Paradigm Shift: Why Dexterous Hands Are Moving Beyond Freedom Degree Competition – Brain Lag & Cost Control Challenges**
  English: Boston Dynamics unveiled a brand new dexterous hand for its humanoid robot Atlas featuring only four fingers: the thumb has 4 degrees of freedom (DoF) and the other three fingers have 3 DoF each, totaling 13 DoF, nearly double the 7 DoF of the previous generation. Other high-DoF examples include the Y-Hand M2 by Yuequan Bionics with 38 DoF, Zhongke Huisi's F-series rope-driven bionic dexterous hand with 37 DoF, and the research version of LinkerHand from Lingxin Qiaoshou with up to 42 DoF. In comparison, the human hand without the wrist has 21 DoF (or 22 with palmar arch micro-movement) and 27 DoF including the wrist. However, enterprises have recently reduced configurations. Unitree Robotics launched its Dex5-S dexterous hand in September with 22 DoF and 22 motors, priced starting from 39,900 yuan. Sino Future Head of Capital Market Fu Yuancheng noted that when DoF exceeds 25, marginal effects decrease while marginal costs rise. Higher DoF hands can cost over 100,000 yuan or millions due to increased motors and sensors. Zhongke Siliconji CEO Zhang Tianyi stated that three-finger hands offer opposing and general gripping with lower cost. Additionally, existing VLA models can only output about 10 motion control vectors at the same timestamp, limiting software capability for 20 to 30 DoF hands.
