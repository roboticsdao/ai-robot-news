Interesting Engineering — Why robot hands are one of the hardest problems in humanoid robotics

Humanoid robots can easily perform backflips because the movement is predictable and the environment can be controlled, whereas household chores like folding laundry or picking up wet sponges involve immense uncertainty as objects bend, slip, and deform. This discrepancy highlights the robotic hand as one of the hardest parts of building a useful humanoid, requiring complex combinations of mechanics, sensing, and control. While a camera can identify an object like an egg, it cannot determine how close the robot is to crushing it, making tactile sensing increasingly crucial. Recent research, such as a 2026 Science Robotics study from Zhejiang University, combined visual and tactile information with reinforcement learning and online imitation learning to achieve an 85% success rate across five complex tasks involving 25 objects. Factory robots benefit from controlled environments and fixed trajectories, unlike homes where physical contact is difficult to model. Chinese robotics companies, including X Square Robot, are actively testing humanoids on household chores such as picking up litter and arranging flowers, though collecting synchronized manipulation data remains a significant challenge.

总结：人形机器人能够轻松完成空翻，是因为该动作可预测且环境可控，而折衣服或捡湿海绵等家务则包含巨大不确定性，物体会弯曲、滑动和变形。 这种差异凸显出机器人手部是制造实用人形机器人最困难的部分之一，需要机械、传感和控制的复杂结合。 虽然摄像头可以识别鸡蛋等物体，但无法确定机器人距离将其捏碎还有多远，这使得触觉传感日益关键。 浙江大学2026年的一项《Science Robotics》研究将视觉和触觉信息与强化学习及在线模仿学习相结合，在涉及25个对象的五项复杂任务中取得了85%的成功率。 工厂机器人受益于可控环境和固定轨迹，这与难以对物理接触进行建模的家庭环境不同。 包括X Square Robot在内的中国机器人公司正在积极测试人形机器人从事捡垃圾和插花等家务，尽管收集同步的操作数据依然是一个重大挑战。
