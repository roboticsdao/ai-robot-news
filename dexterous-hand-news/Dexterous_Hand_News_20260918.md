  English: Humanoid robots can perform complex athletic movements like backflips because they represent predictable, precisely controlled sequences, whereas household chores like folding laundry, picking up wet sponges, or turning keys involve high uncertainty because objects bend, slip, and deform. The human hand features over 30 muscles, 27 joints, and 17,000 sensory receptors, offering high dexterity that vision alone cannot replicate without tactile sensing. A 2026 Science Robotics study from Zhejiang University combined visual and tactile data with reinforcement and online imitation learning to achieve an 85% success rate across 5 complex tasks involving 25 objects, highlighting the critical role of touch in overcoming the data and control bottlenecks of household robotics.
