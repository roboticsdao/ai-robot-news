Robotics & Automation News — Researchers develop 3D-printable humanoid robotic hand costing less than $650

Researchers at the New Jersey Institute of Technology developed a dexterous humanoid robotic hand called the PHD-Hand that can be manufactured using an affordable 3D printer and readily available components for less than $650 in parts. Authored by Neel Adwani, Muhaiminul Islam Akash, Saffia Ouali, Ethan Liu, and Cong Wang, the research paper has been accepted at the IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids 2026). The five-fingered device uses a linkage-driven mechanism with spring steel drive rods and a 3D-printed flexible hinge made of thermoplastic polyurethane at the base of each digit, providing 15 independently actuated degrees of freedom through three actuated degrees of freedom in each finger. The prototype demonstrated all 33 grasp types in the GRASP taxonomy, rotated a 55 mm cube within its palm, repositioned a jar to open its lid, manipulated a precision screwdriver, and adjusted the worm screw of an adjustable wrench. The researchers used Anthropic’s Claude AI system to generate a browser-based control interface and motion trajectories from a textual description, and plan to release CAD models, 3D-printing files, control software, motion sequences, and a bill of materials through an open-source GitHub repository.

总结：新泽西理工学院的研究人员开发了一种名为PHD-Hand的灵巧人形机器人手，使用价格实惠的3D打印机和现成组件制造，零件成本低于$650。 该研究论文由Neel Adwani、Muhaiminul Islam Akash、Saffia Ouali、Ethan Liu和Cong Wang撰写，已被IEEE-RAS人形机器人国际会议（Humanoids 2026）接受。 这个五指装置采用连杆驱动机制，配合弹簧钢驱动杆和在每个手指底部的热塑性聚氨酯3D打印柔性铰链，通过每个手指3个驱动自由度提供了总共15个独立驱动的自由度。 该原型展示了GRASP分类法中的所有33种抓握类型，在其手掌内旋转了55 mm的立方体，重新定位罐子以打开其盖子，操作了精密螺丝刀，并调整了活动扳手的蜗杆螺丝。 研究人员使用Anthropic的Claude AI系统根据文本描述生成了基于浏览器的控制接口和运动轨迹，并计划通过开源GitHub repository发布CAD模型、3D打印文件、控制软件、运动序列和物料清单。

Bioengineer.org — NJIT Engineers Develop Inexpensive 3D-Printed Dexterous Robotic Hand with Modular Digits

Researchers at the New Jersey Institute of Technology designed and built the PHD-Hand, a printable humanoid dexterous robotic hand created by Associate Professor Cong Wang and doctoral student Neel Adwani. The foundational concepts emerged during Wang's 2024 hospital stay, where lack of digital devices led him to focus on mechanical fundamentals and sketch structural designs in a notebook. Upon returning to campus, Wang presented the designs to a multidisciplinary fabrication team including research assistant Muhaiminul Akash, undergraduate researcher Saffia Ouali, and Union County Magnet High School student Ethan Liu. The PHD-Hand features a linkage-driven assembly that eliminates miniature moving parts, allowing production using a budget-grade 3D printer. It incorporates 3D-printed knuckles and joints with internal channels for stabilizing metal rods, modular digits, 3D-printed 2-degree-of-freedom flexure hinges, and springs inside the fingertips. Actuation is driven by standard hobby servos, and the system includes an inherent safety feature where power interruption causes fingers to return to a neutral resting position. Operation and manipulation commands are handled through a web browser-based software interface supporting mouse input and natural language processing.

Interesting Engineering — New 3D-printed robotic hand uses simple parts to replicate human-like movements

Researchers at the New Jersey Institute of Technology, including Associate Professor Cong Wang and doctoral student Neel Adwani, have developed the PHD Hand, a dexterous robotic hand combining 3D-printed components with simple mechanical systems to reduce manufacturing costs and maintenance. The research paper will appear at the IEEE Humanoids conference in December 2026. Wang's design process was inspired by a 2024 hospital stay without digital devices, where a notebook and pen became his tools to rethink robotic manipulation after observing medical professionals perform routine tasks. The PHD Hand uses printed joints and knuckles with narrow internal channels for metal rods, springs inside fingertips, and readily available hobby servos for actuation, avoiding complex tendon-driven mechanisms. If electrical power disappears, the fingers return to a preset neutral position. Users control the hand through browser-based software using a mouse, and the system can accommodate typed or spoken instructions through large language model integration. Adwani's doctoral research will explore manipulating everyday objects in natural ways, such as picking up a spatula from its handle's opposite end, alongside investigating tactile sensing and cameras to recognize objects and coordinate complex movements.

总结：新泽西理工学院的研究人员（包括副教授Cong Wang和博士生Neel Adwani）开发了PHD Hand，这是一种将3D打印组件与简单机械系统相结合的灵巧机器人手，旨在降低制造成本和维护费用。 该研究论文将出现在2026年12月的IEEE Humanoids会议上。 Wang的设计过程灵感来自2024年在没有数码设备的情况下的住院经历，当时在观察医疗专业人员执行常规任务后，笔记本和笔成为他重新思考机器人操纵的工具。 PHD Hand使用带有用于金属棒的狭窄内部通道的打印关节和指关节、指尖内部的弹簧以及现成的爱好舵机进行驱动，避免了复杂的腱驱动机制。 如果电力消失，手指会返回预设的中立位置。 用户通过基于浏览器的软件使用鼠标控制手，并且系统可以通过大语言模型集成适应键入或口头指令。 Adwani的博士研究将探索以自然方式操纵日常物品（例如从铲子手柄的另一端拿起铲子），同时研究触觉传感和相机以识别物体并协调复杂运动。

FabScene — 米ニュージャージー工科大、安価な3Dプリンターで作れるロボットハンドを開発

A research team at the New Jersey Institute of Technology developed the PHD-Hand, an affordable humanoid robot hand made with inexpensive 3D printers and off-the-shelf parts. The hand features five fingers, each moved by three servo motors, and measures about 200mm in length. Published on arXiv on October 21, 2026, and accepted at the Humanoids 2026 conference, the design uses a linkage mechanism instead of tendons. The base joint is 3D printed from soft TPU resin and curves in two directions, reinforced with a 0.6mm stainless steel spring steel rod. Each finger's main components consist of 13 parts for a single-piece print. The team tested the prototype with 33 grasping classifications, successfully performing tasks like turning a precision screwdriver, while tasks requiring narrow finger ranges or lacking a web space were incomplete. Browser-based movement procedures were generated by the Claude AI. Following about 200 hours of testing with two spring steel rod breakages, the team plans to study durability and prepare an open-source release.

总结：美国新泽西理工学院的研究团队开发了名为“PHD-Hand”的人型机器人手，可使用安价的3Dプリンター和市贩部品制作。 该手拥有5根手指，每根手指由3个サーボモーター驱动，长度约为200mm。 该论文于2026年10月21日公开于arXiv，并被Humanoids 2026会议收录。 设计采用连杆机构而非腱绳，指根关节使用TPU树脂3D打印并在中心穿入0.6mm不锈钢弹簧钢棒。 手指主体一体打印仅需13个零件。 团队使用原型机进行了33类抓取测试，完成了旋转精密螺丝刀等操作，部分受限于活动范围的操作未能完全完成。 操作界面由生成式AI Claude生成。 在经历了约200小时及两次弹簧钢棒断裂的测试后，团队计划未来研究耐久性并以开源形式发布设计。
