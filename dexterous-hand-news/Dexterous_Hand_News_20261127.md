- **[2026.11.26] Robotics & Automation News — Researchers develop 3D-printable humanoid robotic hand costing less than $650**
  English: Researchers at the New Jersey Institute of Technology developed a dexterous humanoid robotic hand called the PHD-Hand that can be manufactured using an affordable 3D printer and readily available components for less than $650 in parts. Authored by Neel Adwani, Muhaiminul Islam Akash, Saffia Ouali, Ethan Liu, and Cong Wang, the research paper has been accepted at the IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids 2026). The five-fingered device uses a linkage-driven mechanism with spring steel drive rods and a 3D-printed flexible hinge made of thermoplastic polyurethane at the base of each digit, providing 15 independently actuated degrees of freedom through three actuated degrees of freedom in each finger. The prototype demonstrated all 33 grasp types in the GRASP taxonomy, rotated a 55 mm cube within its palm, repositioned a jar to open its lid, manipulated a precision screwdriver, and adjusted the worm screw of an adjustable wrench. The researchers used Anthropic’s Claude AI system to generate a browser-based control interface and motion trajectories from a textual description, and plan to release CAD models, 3D-printing files, control software, motion sequences, and a bill of materials through an open-source GitHub repository.

Published on arXiv on October 21, 2026, and accepted at the Humanoids 2026 conference, the design uses a linkage mechanism instead of tendons.
