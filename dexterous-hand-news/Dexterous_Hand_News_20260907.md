- **[2026.09.02] Tech Briefs — Nature vs. Nurture with Regards to Robotic Hands**
  English: Researchers in the ValeroLab at the University of Southern California's Viterbi School of Engineering explored whether tactile sensation is strictly necessary for learning to control robotic fingers. Romina Mir, Ali Marjaninejad, Andrew Erwin, and Professor Francisco Valero-Cuevas used computational modeling and machine learning to investigate the relationship between tactile sensors (nature) and training sequence (nurture). Published in Science Advances, their study demonstrated that the sequence of learning, or curriculum, is critical, and a simulated three-finger robotic hand can learn to manipulate objects against gravity with incomplete or absent tactile sensation if the curriculum follows a specific sequence. The findings link machine learning and biology, suggesting that structured rewards guide system development much like biological systems learn from experience.
  中文：总结：南加州大学维特比工程学院ValeroLab的研究人员探讨了触觉感知对于学习控制机器人手指是否绝对必要。 Romina Mir、Ali Marjaninejad、Andrew Erwin和Francisco Valero-Cuevas教授通过计算建模和机器学习，研究了触觉传感器（先天）与训练序列（后天）之间的关系。 他们的研究发表在《科学进展》杂志上，表明学习顺序或课程至关重要；如果课程遵循特定顺序，模拟的三指机器人手即便在触觉不完整或缺失的情况下，也能学会对抗重力操控物体。 这一发现将机器学习与生物学联系起来，表明结构化的奖励能够指导系统发育，就像生物系统从经验中学习一样。
