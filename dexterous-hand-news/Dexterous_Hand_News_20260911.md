  English: Embodied intelligence researcher Chen Tianyi and Yimu Technology founder Eric Li discussed the critical role and current breakthroughs of tactile sensing in robotics. The robotics industry faces a core dilemma: robots can see well, but lack tactile understanding to regulate force, determine stopping points, or distinguish materials, often resulting in stripped screws when relying solely on vision. As of September 2026, the robot tactile sensing industry is approaching a breakout, with Eric Li predicting a milestone by the end of this year or early next year based on humanoid robot mass-production schedules. Tactile information encompasses "force" (vertical pressure, lateral friction, rotational torque) and "morphology" (surface texture, shape, hardness, elasticity). Current sensors use five technology routes, but a hybrid deployment is necessary since whole-body coverage with high-end sensors is cost-prohibitive. Following human anatomy, robots should use high-precision optical sensors on fingertips and lower-cost piezoresistive or capacitive sensors on palms and arms. Yimu Technology's optical sensors achieve a resolution of 240,000 sensing points (about 20 times human fingertips), with procurement costs around 1,000 yuan (approximately $149) that are expected to drop to a few hundred yuan as volume scales. Meanwhile, capacitive solutions cost one-quarter to one-half of optical options and have seen batch deployment in factory sorting and inspection. Additionally, the industry currently suffers from a severe scarcity of tactile data.
