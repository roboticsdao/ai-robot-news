- **[2026.09.02] Tech Briefs — Nature vs. Nurture with Regards to Robotic Hands**
  English: Researchers in the ValeroLab in the Viterbi School of Engineering at USC, including Romina Mir, Ali Marjaninejad, Andrew Erwin, and Professor Francisco Valero-Cuevas, investigated whether tactile sensation is always necessary for learning to control robotic fingers. Using computational modeling and machine learning to simulate a three-finger robotic hand, the team published a paper titled “Curriculum Is More Influential Than Haptic Information During Reinforcement Learning of Object Manipulation Against Gravity” in Science Advances. The study demonstrates that the sequence of learning, or the curriculum, is critical for learning to occur, and that a simulated robotic hand can learn to manipulate objects even with incomplete or absent tactile sensation if the curriculum follows a particular sequence. Co-First Author Mir and Corresponding Author Valero-Cuevas emphasized that reward guides the development of the system, connecting machine learning and biology to help advance artificial intelligence systems.
  中文：总结：南加州大学Viterbi工程学院ValeroLab的研究人员Romina Mir、Ali Marjaninejad、Andrew Erwin和Francisco Valero-Cuevas教授探讨了触觉感知对于学习控制机器人手指是否始终是必要的。 通过使用计算建模和机器学习模拟三指机器人手，该团队在《Science Advances》上发表了一篇题为“Curriculum Is More Influential Than Haptic Information During Reinforcement Learning of Object Manipulation Against Gravity”的论文。 该研究表明，学习顺序（即课程）对于学习的发生至关重要，如果课程遵循特定的顺序，模拟机器人手甚至可以在触觉感知不完整或缺失的情况下学习操作物体。 共同第一作者Mir和通讯作者Valero-Cuevas强调，奖励指导着系统的发展，将机器学习与生物学连接起来，有助于推动人工智能系统的发展。

- **[2026.09.02] PR Newswire — TwinDEX Introduces a Scalable Path from Robot-Free Data Collection to Real-World Dexterous Manipulation**
  English: X Square Robot introduced TwinDEX on September 2, 2026, in Shenzhen, China. TwinDEX is a co-designed, three-finger, nine-DoF dexterous manipulation interface consisting of a wearable device for robot-free data collection and a matched robotic end effector for deployment, sharing seven active and two passive degrees of freedom. The system features synchronized multi-view RGB camera inputs, six-DoF wrist poses, finger joint states, and fingertip tactile signals.
