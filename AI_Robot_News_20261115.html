TechTarget — Agility Robotics first U.S. specialist humanoid maker to go public

Agility Robotics became the first publicly listed humanoid robotics company in the U. S. after completing a business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI, with its common stock trading on Nasdaq under the ticker AGLT. The offering generated about $465 million in capital before expenses, which Agility plans to use to accelerate the commercialization of its Digit humanoid robot. The listing follows the FCC's July initiative to restrict imports of advanced foreign robotic devices. Analysts noted that Agility focuses on manufacturing and logistics, giving it a commercially grounded proposition and about 100 live commercial deployments of Digit robots, unlike competitors targeting domestic applications. However, scaling remains a challenge in integrating humanoids into heavily automated warehouse environments without altering existing infrastructure. Industry experts pointed out that while investors often favor companies promising a fully automated future, Agility must demonstrate that its funding and early deployments translate into repeatable, commercially viable operations through fleet management and warehouse management system integration.

FOX6 News Milwaukee — US humanoid robot production could hit 400,000 units by 2030, JPMorgan says

JPMorgan released a report stating that United States humanoid robot production is expected to reach between 300,000 and 400,000 units by 2030 and could grow to 1.5 million units by 2035. The investment bank attributes this adoption in manufacturing, logistics, and warehousing to labor shortages, improving economics, and rising production capacity as the industry transitions from demonstrations to broader commercial deployment. The report notes that the Trump administration's determination that imported robotics and industrial machinery pose national security risks could lead to tariffs or policy measures aimed at boosting domestic production. Deployment could be constrained by hardware availability, AI training data, computing capacity, and manufacturing output. Apart from Tesla, most US humanoid robot developers target annual production volumes between 10,000 and 30,000 units. The firm highlighted Tesla's Optimus, Agility Robotics' Digit, Boston Dynamics' Atlas, Figure AI's humanoid robots, and Apptronik's Apollo as leading U. S. platforms.

总结：摩根大通发布的一份报告显示，到2030年，美国的人形机器人产量预计将达到30万至40万台，到2035年可能增长至150万台。 这家投资银行表示，随着行业从演示转向更广泛的商业部署，劳动力短缺、不断改善的经济效益和上升的产能正在推动制造业、物流和仓储领域的采用。 报告指出，特朗普政府认定进口机器人和工业机械构成国家安全风险，这可能会引发旨在促进国内生产的关税或其他政策措施。 部署可能会受到硬件可用性、人工智能训练数据、计算能力和制造产出的限制。 除特斯拉外，大多数美国人形机器人开发商的目标年产量在10,000至30,000台之间。 该机构指出，特斯拉的Optimus、Agility Robotics的Digit、波士顿动力公司的Atlas、Figure AI的人形机器人以及Apptronik的Apollo是美国领先的人形机器人平台。

shattered.io — Apple HALO Humanoid Robot: 91% on Unitree G1

Apple researchers detailed a humanoid robot control system called HALO in a paper submitted to arXiv on November 4, 2026. Tested on a commercially available Unitree Robotics G1 humanoid across six previously unseen real rooms, HALO achieved an average success rate of 91% over 360 single-task trials, performing tasks such as obstacle navigation, box pushing, and sitting on furniture. A separate evaluation of chained tasks achieved an average success rate of 70% across 30 trials. The robot carried a Mac mini featuring an M4 Pro chip and 64GB of unified memory for local inference, utilizing Alibaba's Qwen3.6-35B-A3B task-planning model. Training involved approximately 300 hours of simulated human-like motion across roughly 1,000 virtual rooms. The disclosure describes a research result rather than a consumer product or a formal technology partnership with Unitree.

总结：苹果研究人员在2026年11月4日提交至arXiv的一篇论文中详细介绍了一种名为HALO的人形机器人控制系统。 该系统在一台宇树科技的G1市售人形机器人上进行了测试，在6个从未预先扫描过的真实房间中进行了360次单任务试验，平均成功率为91%，任务包括绕过障碍物、推箱子、跟随人员及在家具上坐下等；在30次链式任务评估中平均成功率为70%。 该机器人在机载搭载了一台配备M4 Pro芯片和64GB统一内存的Mac mini进行本地推理，并使用了阿里巴巴开发的Qwen3.6-35B-A3B任务规划模型。 其训练过程涉及在大约1,000个虚拟房间内进行约300小时的模拟类人运动。 该披露内容仅代表一项研究成果，并非消费级产品，也没有确认苹果与宇树科技建立战略合作伙伴关系。

The San Francisco Standard — A SoMa startup is building humanoid robots for war

Foundation, a SoMa-based humanoid robotics startup cofounded in 2024 by Sankaet Pathak, Arjun Sethi, and Mike LeBlanc, develops humanoid robots including the Phantom MK-2, a 200-pound, 6-foot-tall aluminum robot. Initially targeting manufacturing and warehouse automation, the company shifted its focus toward military logistics and weaponry over the course of 2025. In late 2024, Foundation acquired Florida-based Boardwalk Robotics, inheriting approximately $3.2 million in government research contracts, including a project to supply field soldiers. The startup has experienced controversy, including overstated relationships with General Motors, disputed Pentagon and Ukraine claims, and a failure to pay employees following a missed payroll on October 30, 2025.

总结：总部位于旧金山SoMa的具身人形机器人初创公司Foundation由Sankaet Pathak、Arjun Sethi和Mike LeBlanc于2024年共同创立，该公司开发了包括Phantom MK-2在内的人形机器人，这是一款重200磅、高6英尺且由铝制成的机器人。 Foundation最初公开的目标是制造和仓库应用，但在2025年期间将其重点转向了军事后勤和武器装备。 2024年末，Foundation收购了总部位于佛罗里达州的Boardwalk Robotics，继承了约320万美元的政府研究合同，其中包括一项为战场士兵供应物资的机器人研发项目。 该公司面临诸多争议，包括夸大与通用汽车的关系、受到关于五角大楼和乌克兰合同的质疑，以及在2025年10月30日错过发薪日后未能向员工支付薪酬。
