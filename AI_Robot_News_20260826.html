Interesting Engineering — Underwater US robot pulls itself from the ocean without human help

Impossible Metals demonstrated its Eureka II autonomous underwater vehicle successfully recovering itself from the open ocean in Halifax, Nova Scotia, without human intervention. The 10. 5-foot-long, 8,710-pound AUV uses acoustic positioning and computer vision with AprilTag visual markers to locate its surface vessel, attaching via a SmartHook system. Operating at depths up to 19,685 feet, the robot carries a 14. 4 kWh battery and three sets of robotic arms to collect mineral nodules from the seafloor. According to CTO Jason Gilham and executive chairman Steve Curnutte, autonomous recovery reduces operational costs, labor, and personnel risks while supporting responsible ocean access.

总结：Impossible Metals在新斯科舍省哈利法克斯的离岸演示中，展示了其Eureka II自主水下航行器在没有人工干预的情况下成功从公海自行回收。 这款长10. 5英尺、重8,710磅的AUV利用声学定位和带有AprilTag视觉标记的计算机视觉来定位水面船只，并通过SmartHook系统进行连接。 该机器人可在高达19,685英尺的深度作业，携带14. 4千瓦时电池和三套用于从海底收集矿物结核的机械臂。 首席技术官杰森·吉勒姆和执行主席史蒂夫·库尔努特表示，自主回收可降低运营成本、劳动力和人员风险，同时支持负责任的海洋开发。
